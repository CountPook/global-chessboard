COVER STORY · ISSUE #8

The United States Completes Its Military Withdrawal From Iraq

Est. read time: 4 min · Middle East · Security

On September 30 the United States Central Command said the last American forces and their equipment had departed Erbil Air Base in northern Iraq, ending a military mission that began in 2014 to fight the Islamic State group. The move closes Operation Inherent Resolve inside Iraq and follows a transition plan that Washington and Baghdad announced in 2024, shifting primary responsibility for the country's security to Iraqi forces. It also draws a line under a broader American military presence that stretched across more than two decades and the 2003 invasion that preceded it. The departure lands at a sensitive moment, with Kurdish authorities voicing concern about their defenses and Iran aligned factions casting the exit as a victory.

One Perspective

Supporters of the withdrawal argue that the mission had achieved its core purpose. The Islamic State lost the territory it once held, and Iraq's army and counterterrorism units have led operations for years. From this view a continued foreign troop presence had become a political liability in Baghdad, where factions aligned with Tehran repeatedly demanded an exit and framed the Americans as occupiers. Many in the Iraqi government present the handover as a mark of restored sovereignty and a normal step for a state that can now manage its own security.

Another Perspective

Critics counter that the departure is premature and risks reopening space for the Islamic State to regroup. Kurdish officials in the north, who long treated the American presence as a stabilizing factor, have raised concerns about gaps in their defenses and about disputes with Baghdad over budgets and territory. Skeptics also warn that the exit strengthens the hand of Iran aligned militias and reduces Washington's ability to respond quickly to threats crossing from neighboring Syria, where instability persists. In this reading the timing reflects political pressure more than conditions on the ground.

The Strategic Reality

Analysts generally describe the Islamic State as degraded but not eliminated, with cells still active in parts of Iraq and Syria. The United States has signaled that it will keep a smaller regional footprint and rely on Iraqi forces, intelligence sharing, and what officials call over the horizon strike capabilities if needed. The withdrawal also feeds a longer contest over influence in Iraq between Washington and Tehran that predates this decision and will outlast it. The 2011 drawdown, which preceded the group's rise in 2014, is part of why observers are watching Iraqi capacity and regional spillover closely.

ECONOMICS · EAST ASIA

Washington and Beijing Cut Tariffs on Thirty Billion Dollars in Goods

Est. read time: 4 min · East Asia · Trade

Following a summit between President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping, the United States and China agreed to cut tariffs on roughly thirty billion dollars in goods and to extend a trade truce that had been set to expire on November 10 by two more months. Officials released detailed product lists, covering more than sixteen hundred categories of American exports to China, from farm commodities to timber and medical equipment, and a smaller set of Chinese exports to the United States that includes items such as fireworks, glassware, and holiday ornaments. The two sides also agreed to open a dialogue on artificial intelligence, including a channel for handling AI related incidents, while Beijing committed to buy a large volume of American coal. The deal eases pressure in the most consequential economic relationship in the world while leaving the hardest questions for later.

One Perspective

Supporters frame the agreement as a welcome de-escalation that offers immediate relief to exporters and consumers on both sides. For American farmers and manufacturers, lower Chinese tariffs reopen access to a vast market, while reduced duties on Chinese consumer goods can ease prices for households. Backers also point to the new artificial intelligence channel as a prudent guardrail between the two leading powers in the field, and they argue that extending the truce buys time to negotiate a broader and more durable settlement. In this view, incremental steps that keep both governments talking are preferable to a renewed tariff spiral.

Another Perspective

Skeptics argue that the agreement is modest and largely symbolic set against the full scope of trade between the two economies. Thirty billion dollars is a small share of annual flows, they note, and the deal leaves untouched the central disputes over advanced technology controls, industrial subsidies, and market access that drive the rivalry. Some critics on both sides worry that a short extension of the truce simply postpones confrontation without changing its direction, and others question whether commitments such as coal purchases will be fully honored. From this angle the summit produced a pause rather than a turning point.

The Strategic Reality

Analysts tend to read the deal as a tactical easing within a long running strategic competition rather than a reversal of it. The structural forces pushing Washington and Beijing to reduce their dependence on each other, from supply chain security to export controls on advanced chips, remain in place and are likely to intensify. The artificial intelligence channel is a notable first step, though its value will depend on whether it functions in a genuine crisis. For now, markets and trading partners are treating the agreement as a signal that both capitals want to manage the rivalry rather than let it escalate, even as the underlying contest continues.

POLITICS · NORTH AFRICA

Morocco Names Its First Woman Prime Minister

Est. read time: 3 min · North Africa · Politics

On September 29 King Mohammed VI appointed Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri as Morocco's first woman prime minister and asked her to form a new government. The appointment followed parliamentary elections on September 23 in which her party, the monarchy aligned Authenticity and Modernity Party, won the largest share of seats, taking 97 of the 395 seats in the lower house. El Mansouri, a lawyer who previously served as the first female mayor of Marrakesh and as housing minister, becomes the third woman to lead a government in the Arab world. The milestone is historic in a country where the head of government has always been a man, even as ultimate authority continues to rest with the monarchy.

One Perspective

Those who welcome the appointment describe it as a genuine advance for women's representation in Moroccan public life and across the region. They point to El Mansouri's record in municipal government and as a minister as evidence that she brings administrative experience rather than a purely symbolic profile. Supporters argue that placing a woman at the head of government sends a signal about modernization and can encourage broader participation by women in politics. For this camp the moment carries real weight regardless of the constraints of the office.

Another Perspective

Others caution that the significance should not be overstated given how power is distributed in Morocco. Under the constitution the king retains decisive authority over security, religion, and major strategic questions, and the prime minister governs within those limits. Because El Mansouri's party is closely aligned with the palace, skeptics question how much independent room she will have to set policy, and they note that building a stable coalition from 97 seats will require bargaining with rival parties. In this reading the appointment is meaningful but operates inside a system where the monarchy remains the central actor.

The Strategic Reality

Analysts describe Morocco as a hybrid system in which an elected government manages daily affairs while the king holds ultimate power, a structure that shapes how much any prime minister can achieve. The incoming government faces pressing economic challenges, including youth unemployment, regional inequality, and water stress worsened by recurring drought. On foreign policy continuity is widely expected, with Rabat maintaining its position on Western Sahara and its close ties with Europe, the United States, and Gulf states. The test for El Mansouri will be whether she can turn a historic appointment into tangible results within the boundaries of the office.

Sources: Al Jazeera, CNN, Arab News

READER FAQ

Your Questions, Answered

Each issue we answer the most pressing questions from our readers. Submit yours by replying to this email.

Q: Why do countries withdraw troops even when a threat has not fully disappeared?

A: Military deployments carry political, financial, and strategic costs that governments weigh against the remaining threat. A presence once seen as essential can become a domestic liability for the host country and an open ended commitment for the sending one. Leaders often shift from large deployments to smaller footprints that rely on local forces, intelligence sharing, and the ability to strike from a distance, judging that the benefits of staying no longer outweigh the costs. The risk is that an early exit lets a weakened adversary recover, which is why such decisions are closely debated.

Q: What is a trade truce, and why do governments set expiration dates on them?

A: A trade truce is a temporary agreement to pause or roll back tariffs and other barriers while negotiators work toward a broader deal. Governments attach expiration dates to keep pressure on both sides to keep talking, since an open ended pause can remove the incentive to compromise. Extending a truce, as Washington and Beijing just did, signals that both want to avoid escalation but have not yet resolved the core disputes. The approach lets each side claim progress while preserving leverage for the next round.

Q: In a constitutional monarchy, how is power divided between a king and a prime minister?

A: Arrangements vary widely, but in many constitutional monarchies the monarch serves as head of state while an elected prime minister heads the government and manages daily policy. In some countries the monarch's role is largely ceremonial, while in others, as in Morocco, the king retains substantial authority over areas such as defense, foreign affairs, and religion. The balance is set by the constitution and by political practice, and it determines how much independent power a prime minister actually holds. Reading that balance is key to understanding what a change in government does and does not mean.
